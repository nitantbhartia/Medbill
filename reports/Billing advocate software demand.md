# Advocates will pay, but not $499

Independent US medical billing advocates do pay for tools, but only a little: the documented spend is a few hundred dollars a year on directory memberships, recertification and, for some, a $10-66/month coding reference. Nothing in the evidence shows a solo advocate paying $99-499/month per seat for anything. The pool of buyers is also small. Industry estimates put independent US advocates at roughly **500-1,500 practitioners**, perhaps **300-500 of them full-time**, and billing specialists are only a minority of that group. Even if every one of them bought a $99/month seat, the result is a business of under $2M ARR. Realistic adoption puts it in the low hundreds of thousands. The niche is almost empty: the only advocate-labeled case-management product found is Vitafy's VSuite, and its traction is unknown. The empty niche looks more like a sign of a small, price-sensitive market than an opportunity nobody has noticed. The verdict for BillKarma is **no-go on a $99-499/month per-seat SaaS for independent advocate firms as the core business**. The same feature set (Medicare-benchmark bill audit, dispute templates, case and outcome tracking) looks more promising sold to organizations that employ many advocates or that pay for claim savings: Medicare-reimbursed advocacy platforms, consumer bill-negotiation services, and self-funded employer plans and TPAs. One caveat: the research session could not open most advocate-industry sites and found no first-hand advocate forum posts. Several counts below rest on search snippets and need to be checked directly before a final decision.

## A buyer pool measured in hundreds, not thousands

The field is unlicensed, so no official count exists ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-coding-billing-and-collections/patient-advocates-guide-through-confusion-of-medical-bills/)). The best series comes from Trisha Torrey and APHA. They estimated **150-200 private-practice advocates in 2012**, plus another 250-300 who were part-time or between jobs ([APHA blog, 2012](https://aphablog.com/2012/04/16/just-how-many-patient-advocates-are-there/)). By 2018 the estimate was **300-500 independent practices** ([APHA blog, 2018](https://aphablog.com/2018/10/23/successes-failures-and-my-biggest-surprise/)), and by around 2020 it was an "educated guesstimate" of **500 or more** privately paid advocates ([APHA](https://aphadvocates.org/profession-overview/)). The largest current proxy is Greater National Advocates' free directory, with **more than 1,000 advocate profiles**. In 2025 it handled about 200,000 searches and connected more than 120,000 calls to advocates ([Middletown Life, 2026](https://lifestyle.middletownlifemagazine.com/story/808245/no-one-should-navigate-healthcare-alone-not-even-patient-advocates/)). Listing is free, so that figure includes part-timers and dormant profiles.

Credentials tell the same story. The Board Certified Patient Advocate credential grew quickly, from 149 holders in 2018 to 893 in April 2021 ([PACB](https://www.pacboard.org/2021/04/13/spring21/)). Growth then slowed to **1,040 by May 2022**, the last public count ([PACB](https://www.pacboard.org/2022/05/27/bcpa-exam-results-spring-2022/)). Many of those holders work as employees at hospitals, insurers or nonprofits rather than running independent practices. Billing specialists are a subset of an already small group. The one billing- and claims-specific association, ACAP, once listed **just 33 members in 16 states** ([claims.org](https://claims.org/pdf/De_mystifying_health_bills.pdf)). GNA does offer an "Insurance & Billing" filter, but the research could not count its listings ([GNA](https://gnanow.org/advocates?search_type=speciality_id&speciality_id=16)).

Putting these sources together, my working estimate is about **500-1,500 independent advocates in total, 300-500 full-time practices, and perhaps 75-450 advocates who focus on billing**. Only about 5-15 consumer billing-advocacy firms have 10 or more staff. Two examples are Goodbill, which has raised $5.3M ([Goodbill](https://www.goodbill.com/blog/news-goodbill-raise-2m-funding-expands-cost-containment-plans-tpas)), and Resolve, which raised a $3.3M seed round ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220718005136/en/Resolve-Raises-3.3M-in-Seed-Funding)). Those are inferences, not measurements. They are still enough to bound the opportunity: 1,500 seats at $99/month is about $1.8M ARR at 100% penetration. A more realistic 10-20% uptake of the full-time segment, at a price advocates can bear, produces roughly **$50K-250K ARR**.

## Solo advocates gross tens of thousands and price per case

Advocates earn money in lumpy amounts tied to individual cases. Contingency is the most common model for billing work, usually **25-35% of savings**, though the full range runs from 15% to 50% ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/advocate-sidebar); [CareRoute](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/medical-bill-negotiation-cost)). Hourly rates cluster at **$100-200**, and nurse-credentialed advocates command $150-250 ([Baba](https://www.baba.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-patient-advocate/)). Flat fees run **$300-1,500** for a bounded task such as one bill review or one appeal ([Pinnie](https://www.pinnie.com/articles/how-much-does-a-medical-billing-advocate-cost)). One comparison site puts the average successful case at about **$1,500 saved** ([CareRoute](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/compare-medical-bill-negotiation-services)). At 25-35% contingency, that means a typical case brings in roughly $375-525 in fees.

No association has published advocate income data. The only practitioner survey found dates from 2016. It reported that advocates were satisfied and earning "enough," and described independents as **older, female, highly educated career-changers**, many with prior medical careers ([APHA, 2016](https://aphablog.com/2016/07/11/survey-says-the-results-are-in/)). The closest available proxies are wages for employed medical billing advocates, which average **$42,673** ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Medical-Billing-Advocate-Salary)), and median revenue for solo therapists, which was **$75,000** in 2025 ([Heard](https://www.joinheard.com/resources/the-heard-2026-financial-state-of-private-practice-report)). Based on these, I estimate a solo billing advocate grosses somewhere between about $20K part-time and $100K once established. On those numbers, a $99/month tool costs **1-6% of revenue**, which is defensible if it saves an hour a month or wins one more case. A $499/month seat costs **6-30% of revenue**, more than most of these practices can carry. Startup budgets of **$2K-15K** in total ([Startup Financial Projection](https://startupfinancialprojection.com/blogs/opening/patient-advocacy); [Workhint](https://blog.workhint.com/blog/start-patient-advocacy-business-no-staff/)) confirm that this is a low-overhead, home-based trade.

## Today's tool stack is free, generic, or under $66 a month

No survey lists what advocates actually use day to day. One advocate-facing guide notes that independent advocates are generally **not HIPAA-covered entities** and can run on paid Google Workspace, cloud storage with two-factor authentication, and a password manager ([Tennessee Health Advocates](https://tennesseehealthadvocates.com/independent-patient-advocate-hipaa-guide/)). That removes compliance as a reason they have to buy anything. For bill audits, advocates have free benchmark sources such as FAIR Health Consumer ([FAIR Health](https://www.fairhealthconsumer.org/insurance-basics/your-costs/negotiating-your-costs)) and the CMS fee schedule. Coding references sold to provider-side coders are the paid option. The documented price anchors are below.

| Spend item | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| APHA Premium +ADL (directory listing) | $289 first year, $259/yr renewal | [APHA](https://aphadvocates.org/memberships/premium-adl/) |
| GNA directory listing | Free, "always free for advocates" | [GNA](https://gnanow.org/professionals-join-here.html) |
| BCPA recertification (every 3 yrs) | $195-295 | [PACB](https://www.pacboard.org/stay-certified/) |
| AAPC Codify | $10-50/mo (GetApp); up to $66/mo non-member | [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/healthcare-pharmaceuticals-software/a/codify); [hccbuddy](https://hccbuddy.com/compare/hcc-buddy-vs-codify) |
| Find-A-Code basic | ~$45/mo | [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/software/medical-coding-computer-assisted-coding-cac/icd-10-cmpcs-encoders/find-a-code) |
| HIPAA case management (CharityTracker) | $20/mo, Pro $60/user/mo | [CharityTracker](https://www.charitytracker.com/feeds/blog/hipaa-compliant-case-management-software) |
| SimplePractice (solo therapists, analog) | $49 / $79 / $99 per month | [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/pricing-guides/simplepractice-pricing) |
| AI appeal letter (Claimable, consumer) | $39.95 per appeal | [Healthcare Brew](https://www.healthcare-brew.com/stories/2024/12/17/startup-appealing-insurance-denials-patients-behalf) |

The most relevant comparison is SimplePractice. Solo therapists treat it as mission-critical because they bill insurance every week for recurring clients, and they pay **$49-99/month**. SimplePractice raised its Starter plan from $29 to $49 in 2025 ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/simplepractice/diffs/2025Q1)). Advocates work episodic cases and never submit claims, so their need is weaker. Their willingness to pay should sit at or below the $49 Starter tier, not at $99-499. There are two positive signals. Solo clinicians pay **$35/month** for a separate AI note-taker add-on ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/telehealth/simplepractice/hidden-costs/)), which suggests an AI dispute-letter generator could sell as a paid module. Care-management vendors also charge per client, for example **$9.99 per care recipient per month** ([Agensy](https://agensy.com/pricing.html)), a model that fits advocates' uneven caseloads better than a flat seat price.

## Funded competitors are selling around advocates, not to them

Almost no vendor treats independent advocates as its customer. The only advocate-labeled product found is **VSuite by Vitafy**, a HIPAA case-management tool "for healthcare advocates" whose pricing and traction could not be verified ([Vitafy](https://www.vitafyhealth.com/vsuite/for-healthcare-advocates)). Venture money has gone elsewhere, into products that compete with advocates. **Medbill AI** raised a **$16.3M seed** in July 2024 to automate medical billing for consumers ([VCBacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/company/medbill-ai)). That name overlap with this project also matters for brand and search. **Sheer Health** raised about $16.5M, including a $13M Series A, for a consumer bill-and-claims app ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/516159-55)). **Avelis Health** (YC Summer 2025, $500K) uses AI agents to audit and negotiate consumer bills ([People of Color in Tech](https://peopleofcolorintech.com/articles/y-combinator-invests-500k-in-ai-startup-helping-americans-save-money-on-medical-bills/)). On the appeals side, **Counterforce Health** offers a free, grant-funded AI appeal generator ([Stateline, 2025](https://stateline.org/2025/11/20/patients-deploy-bots-to-battle-health-insurers-that-deny-care/)). **Fight Health Insurance** is a free, open-source appeal tool, and its paid "Fight Paperwork" version is sold to practices and health systems rather than advocates ([Managed Healthcare Executive](https://managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/power-to-the-patients-ai-for-fighting-insurance-denials)).

This has two consequences for BillKarma. First, dispute templates and letter generation are effectively free now, or cost $39.95 per use, so they cannot carry a subscription price by themselves. Paid value has to come from the Medicare-benchmark audit and from case and outcome tracking. Second, free consumer AI tools take the simple denials and small bills away from human advocates. That squeezes the low end of the advocates' own client base, which is the part of the market BillKarma would otherwise sell to.

The history of the space shows the same pattern repeatedly: companies move from consumers to institutions. Remedy failed because provider billing offices refused to work with third parties ([Fast Company, 2017](https://www.fastcompany.com/40483774/remedy-wanted-to-cut-peoples-medical-bills-but-the-health-care-system-wouldnt-let-it)). DisputeBills shut down. Simplee pivoted from a consumer app to hospital software ([MedCity News, 2017](https://medcitynews.com/2017/11/medical-billing-startups/)). Goodbill caps its consumer fee at $1,000 ([Goodbill Help](https://help.goodbill.com/en/articles/254209)) and in 2024 moved toward self-funded plans and TPAs, covering about 50,000 people ([GeekWire, 2024](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/goodbill-raises-2m-and-expands-customer-base-to-unearth-and-challenge-medical-billing-errors/)). No post-mortem was found for software sold specifically to advocates, which most likely means no funded company has tried it.

## Red flags outweigh the open niche

Several risks compound. First, the free GNA directory and the low-hundreds-per-year spending pattern anchor advocates toward free tools. Second, gig-style advocate platforms pay contractors **$20-25/hour** ([ZipRecruiter Solace listing](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Solace-Rn-Healthcare-Advocate/-in-Houston,TX); [Understood Care](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/understoodcare/jobs/4109765009)), and those workers use the platform's own tools. Third, HIPAA generally does not apply when a patient hires an advocate directly ([AccountableHQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/business-associate-agreement-requirements-for-patient-billing-advocate-firms)), so there is no regulatory reason to buy. Fourth, independent income is unpredictable: "a slow year will mean a lower income" ([Vault](https://legacy.vault.com/industries-professions/professions/h/health-advocates/earnings)), which drives churn on any fixed monthly fee. Fifth, the buyer profile of older, late-career solo operators suggests self-serve SaaS will spread mainly by peer referral, which is slow.

The best case for the niche is that no strong incumbent exists and per-case revenue can be meaningful: 25% of a $4,000 saving is $1,000. Consumer guides also warn clients to define "billable savings" in writing ([lowerhospitalbill.com](https://lowerhospitalbill.com/medical-bill-advocate/)). That points to a real need for savings documentation and outcome audit trails, which a tracking product could meet. These are reasons to sell a cheap tier to advocates, not reasons to build the company around them.

The alternative buyers are larger and already pay per case or per member. **Solace** bills Medicare for advocacy ([Solace](https://www.solace.health/care/a-better-healthcare-experience)) and employs many advocates, so per-case efficiency improves its margins directly. Consumer negotiation services such as Resolve charge **25% of savings with a $1,000 minimum** on bills under $15,000 ([Resolve](https://resolvemedicalbills.com/faqs/how-much-does-it-cost-to-work-with-resolve)) and run teams of human advocates. Employer navigation vendors operate at very different scale. Transcarent bought Accolade for about **$621M**, and the combined company serves more than 20M members ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/transcarent-acquire-accolade-621-million-health-benefits-navigation/736798/)). Health Advocate has historically been priced around **$2.25 PEPM** ([RFP.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare/health-navigation-solutions/health-advocate/included-health)). Self-funded plans and TPAs are the segment Goodbill moved toward. These organizations may build their own tooling, and enterprise sales cycles are slow, but a single contract with them could match the revenue from the entire solo-advocate market.

## Conclusion

The question is less whether advocates will pay and more who captures the savings. A solo advocate takes about a third of a typical $1,500 saving, so a tool's value to them tops out at tens of dollars per case. An organization paid per member, per Medicare encounter or per dollar of claims savings captures the whole saving at volume. That is where a Medicare-benchmark audit engine is worth $499 a seat or more. **Go/no-go: no-go** on a $99-499/month advocate-firm SaaS as the primary business. **Conditional go** on building the audit engine for institutional buyers, with advocates served by a low-priced tier of about $29-49/month or per-case pricing. That tier would act as a distribution and data channel rather than the main source of revenue.

I recommend the following next steps, in order. First, close the evidence gap cheaply: count the GNA "Insurance & Billing" listings against the full directory and pull 15-20 advocate rate cards. Second, run 10-15 discovery calls and a paid pre-sale, for example 10 founding advocates at $39/month, to test real willingness to pay rather than inferred willingness. Third, in parallel, open conversations with Solace, Understood Care, Resolve and two or three TPAs or self-funded employers about piloting the bill audit on their case volume. Medical-debt and consumer attorneys, who already pay for legal SaaS, are an untested secondary segment worth one round of interviews. Fourth, resolve the Medbill/Medbill AI name collision before investing in brand.
