# Willingness to Pay for Software Among US Medical Billing / Patient Advocates, Plus Red Flags

> Method note (2026-11-16): The session's network blocked direct page fetches for most advocate-industry domains (aphadvocates.org, gnanow.org, pacboard.org, theberylinstitute.org, simplepractice.com all failed DNS resolution). Every finding below comes from web-search result snippets, not full-page reads. Exact quotes are snippet text. Re-verify key figures on the primary pages before relying on them. No Reddit, Facebook-group or LinkedIn threads came up in searches (three attempts), so first-hand advocate voice is the largest gap.

## Do association surveys (PACB, GNA, ACAP, NAHAC, AdvoConnection/APHA) report on advocate tech spending, pain points, or tool needs?

### Takeaway
I found no association survey that measures advocate software or tech spending. The only practitioner survey found is about 10 years old (APHA, 2016). It reports that advocates are satisfied and earning "enough." It also profiles independent advocates as older, female, highly educated career-changers, which suggests late-career solo operators rather than tech-forward buyers.

### Cited Findings
- **APHA "National Health and Patient Advocate Survey" (July 2016).** The headline finding was that independent and employed advocates earn enough and are satisfied enough to keep doing the work. "Advocates reported that they are highly satisfied with the work." Privately paid independent advocates "tended to be older, white, female, more highly educated, and to have other medical training or prior careers in related fields." Advocates were "optimistic that the field of advocacy will grow in the next five years." The search snippet did not include income figures. — [APHA blog, "Survey Says! The Results Are In" (2016)](https://aphablog.com/2016/07/11/survey-says-the-results-are-in/); [myapha.org mirror](https://myapha.org/survey-says-the-results-are-in/)
- **Beryl Institute, "Establishing Evidence-Based Benchmarks & Guidelines for the Field of Patient Advocacy: Survey Results."** This is a paid product and its date is unknown. Beryl's audience is hospital-employed patient-experience and patient-relations staff, not independent billing advocates. I could not open the page. — [Beryl Institute](https://theberylinstitute.org/product/establishing-evidence-based-benchmarks-guidelines-for-the-field-of-patient-advocacy-survey-results/)
- **Greater National Advocates (GNA).** GNA runs a free national directory. Its site says the service "will always be free for advocates," with no upsell or hidden charge. Listing requires agreeing to the Collaborative Code of Ethics. No survey data was found. — [GNA join page](https://gnanow.org/professionals-join-here.html) (snippet only; date unknown)
- **AdvoConnection / APHA.** This is a membership organization for independent advocates. It offers a directory, marketing support and business teleconferences. Its directory now appears under the name "Umbra Health Advocacy (formerly AdvoConnection)." — [healthadvocateprograms.com listing](https://healthadvocateprograms.com/listings/organizations/alliance.htm); [APHA Premium +ADL page](https://aphadvocates.org/memberships/premium-adl/)
- **NAHAC and ACAP.** Searches for NAHAC dues or surveys returned only unrelated organizations (housing co-ops, NACHC). Nothing was found for ACAP. — search on "NAHAC membership dues health advocate annual" (no relevant results)

### Inferences
- Because no association tracks tech spending, either the associations don't see software as a member priority or the market is too small to survey. Either way, willingness to pay is unproven, not disproven.
- The demographic profile (older career-changers, often ex-nurses or ex-billers) points to a buyer who wants simple tools and support, and who is unlikely to try self-serve SaaS without a peer recommendation.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 survey of independent advocates on income, caseload, hours or tools was found.
- No pain-point data (case tracking, appeal deadlines, CPT lookups, letter writing) from any association.
- I could not read the PACB, Beryl or GNA pages directly because the network blocked them.

## Forum, Reddit, Facebook-group and LinkedIn discussions of tools, pain points and price sensitivity

### Takeaway
Searches turned up no first-hand forum discussion. What exists publicly is consumer-facing content about what advocates charge (15–35% contingency, $75–$350/hr). I found no advocate-specific case-management software. The nearest analogs are generic nonprofit or HIPAA case-management tools priced from $20/user/mo.

### Cited Findings
- **Advocate pricing to consumers (2025–2026 pages).** Contingency is "typically" 15%–35% of savings, and another source gives 25–33% as the standard band. Hourly rates run "$75 to $350." "The broader market runs from roughly 15 to 50 percent." — [lowerhospitalbill.com (2026)](https://lowerhospitalbill.com/medical-bill-advocate/); [medadvocate.net guide](https://www.medadvocate.net/guides/medical-bill-advocate/); [medicalbillrescue.com](https://medicalbillrescue.com/resources/medical-billing-advocates); [dearestcare.com](https://www.dearestcare.com/en/blog/medical-billing-advocate-cost)
- **Solace (Aug 2025).** A Solace article says independent advocates "typically charge between $75-$300 per hour or offer monthly retainers from $200-$1,000." — [Solace, "How To Get A Patient Advocate" (updated Aug 2025)](https://www.solace.health/articles/how-to-get-a-patient-advocate)
- **Unsourced income claim.** One career guide says independent advocates "earn $80,000–$150,000 depending on caseload and specialty." It gives no source, so treat it as weak evidence. — [usacareerfinder.com](https://www.usacareerfinder.com/careers/patient-advocate.html)
- **Software-category search.** Searches for advocate case-tracking software returned only provider billing systems (AdvancedMD, DrChrono, Tebra, CareCloud) and nonprofit or human-services case management. "Nothing in these results targets patient advocates or bill-dispute work." — [G2 medical billing](https://www.g2.com/tags/medical-billing-software); [GetApp patient case management](https://www.getapp.com/healthcare-pharmaceuticals-software/patient-case-management/f/medical-billing/)
- **Nearest-analog pricing for case-management tools (vendor blogs, 2026).**
  - CharityTracker (HIPAA case management) "starts at $20/mo," with a Pro tier at "$60/month per user."
  - LiveImpact is "flat-rate starting around $350/month."
  - Patient Protect (HIPAA compliance, not case tracking) costs "$39/month per office."
  - Sources: [CharityTracker blog](https://www.charitytracker.com/feeds/blog/hipaa-compliant-case-management-software); [LiveImpact blog](https://www.liveimpact.org/blog/hipaa-compliant-case-management-software); [Patient Protect](https://patient-protect.com/post/affordable-hipaa-compliance-software-small-practices)

### Inferences
- Revenue per case can be meaningful. For example, 25% of $4,000 in savings is $1,000 on a single case. So a tool that saves an hour per case or recovers one extra case a month could justify $50–$100/mo. That is arithmetic, not observed WTP.
- The apparent lack of any advocate-specific practice-management product suggests one of two things: an open niche, or a market too small or too price-sensitive to have sustained one. Prior attempts may have existed and failed. I found no evidence either way.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, Facebook-group or LinkedIn quotes were found. Searches returned vendor pages only. Direct browsing of r/medicalbilling, r/MedicalBillingAndCoding and r/HealthInsurance, plus the APHA/Umbra and Facebook advocate groups, is needed.
- No data on which tools advocates use today (spreadsheets, Google Drive, CRMs, generic practice-management software).

## Recurring costs advocates already pay (willingness-to-pay anchors)

### Takeaway
The documented recurring spend is small: about $259–$289/yr for APHA/Umbra membership with a directory listing, $195–$295 every 3 years for BCPA recertification, and a free GNA listing. Those who also hold coding credentials may pay about $175–$222/yr for AAPC and $10–$50/mo for Codify. Together this suggests a total professional-tools budget in the low hundreds of dollars per year for many advocates.

### Cited Findings
- **APHA (AdvoConnection/Umbra directory).** "A first year APHA Premium (or Premium +ADL) Membership is $289. Premium renewal = $259 per year." A directory listing requires the +ADL tier. The page was about 2.5 years old at search time, so roughly 2024. An older third-party listing gives dues of "$59 to $289 per year." — [APHA Premium +ADL](https://aphadvocates.org/memberships/premium-adl/); [healthadvocateprograms.com](https://healthadvocateprograms.com/listings/organizations/alliance.htm)
- **GNA directory.** It is free, with "no upsell or hidden charge." — [GNA](https://gnanow.org/professionals-join-here.html)
- **PACB / BCPA fees (date not shown in snippet).**
  - Exam reschedule: $125
  - Retest: $275
  - Recertification with continuing education: $195
  - Recertification by exam: $295
  - Renewal is every 3 years.
  - The initial exam fee was not in the snippet.
  - Sources: [PACB Get Certified](https://www.pacboard.org/get-certified/); [PACB Stay Certified](https://www.pacboard.org/stay-certified/)
- **AAPC membership.** Third-party sources put it at "$175 to $200 per year" or "$222/year." The sources conflict and neither is official. — [cpc-prep.pages.dev (2026)](https://cpc-prep.pages.dev/medical-coding-certification-cost/); [practicetestgeeks.com (2026)](https://practicetestgeeks.com/aapc/aapc-codify)
- **AAPC Codify (GetApp).** Monthly tiers are "Coder Search 10 per month, Basic Coder 29 per month, Pro Fee Coder 40 per month, Complete Coder 50 per month." Another source lists Complete Coder at "$626 annually." — [GetApp Codify](https://www.getapp.com/healthcare-pharmaceuticals-software/a/codify); [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/software/medical-coding-computer-assisted-coding-cac/icd-10-cmpcs-encoders/codify-by-aapc)

### Inferences
- Codify at $10–$50/mo is the closest proof that billing-oriented solo workers pay monthly for a reference or lookup tool. A CPT/pricing-lookup feature priced near $29–$50/mo would sit within an existing spending habit.
- Directory listings are the main paid tool today, and their value is lead generation. A product that brings clients may command more than one that only improves workflow.

### Gaps
- Professional liability (E&O) insurance premiums for advocates were not found.
- No figure for total annual tech or tool spend per advocate.
- The initial BCPA exam fee was not found.

## Adjacent analogs: what solo professionals pay for vertical SaaS

### Takeaway
Solo therapists pay $49–$99/mo for SimplePractice, and SimplePractice raised its Starter plan from $29 to $49 in 2025 without visible collapse. Care-management software is often priced per client, for example $9.99 per care recipient per month. These figures set a ceiling of roughly $50–$100/mo for a solo practice-management tool. Advocates likely sit at the lower end, given lower volume and the absence of insurance billing needs.

### Cited Findings
- **SimplePractice list prices (2026).** "Starter $49, Essential $79 and Plus $99 monthly." A conflicting source lists Essential at $69/mo (Toolradar, Sept 2026), and the $79 figure is more widely reported. — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/pricing-guides/simplepractice-pricing); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/telehealth/simplepractice/hidden-costs/); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/simplepractice/pricing)
- **SimplePractice price increase (Jan–Apr 2025).** "Starter plan increased from $29/month to $49/month (69% increase)." — [PricingSaaS 2025Q1 diff](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/simplepractice/diffs/2025Q1)
- **SimplePractice add-ons.** The AI Note Taker is "$35/month per clinician." ePrescribe is "$49/month per clinician" plus an $89 setup fee. Payments cost "3.15% + $0.30." — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/telehealth/simplepractice/hidden-costs/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/130710/SimplePractice/pricing/)
- **Care-management software.** Agensy charges "$9.99 per care recipient, per month," with unlimited users. ShiftCare charges "$8 per user a month, billed annually" for its base tier. — [Agensy pricing](https://agensy.com/pricing.html); [ShiftCare](https://shiftcare.com/)
- **Aging Life Care software.** I found no prices for tools used by Aging Life Care professionals (e.g., CareNote). — search on "Aging Life Care Association care manager software pricing" (no direct results)

### Inferences
- Therapists bill insurance weekly and have recurring clients, so the practice-management tool is mission-critical for them. Advocates handle episodic cases with no claims submission, so their need is weaker. Expect advocate WTP to be at or below SimplePractice Starter ($49).
- The AI-scribe add-on at $35/mo shows solo clinicians will pay separately for AI document generation. An AI appeal-letter or dispute-letter generator is a plausible paid add-on by analogy.
- Per-case pricing (like Agensy's per-recipient model) may fit advocates' variable, episodic caseloads better than a flat subscription.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch SimplePractice or Practice Better pricing pages directly.
- No pricing was found for software used by independent insurance agents or solo bookkeepers in this session.

## Red flags for selling SaaS to advocates

### Takeaway
Several structural risks stand out:
- The market is small, fragmented and often part-time.
- The main association directory (GNA) is free, which anchors toward free tools.
- Gig platforms pay advocates $20–$25/hr as contractors, which caps tool budgets.
- Free AI appeal tools (Counterforce, Fight Health Insurance) and freemium apps (Sheer) let consumers do it themselves, shrinking advocates' simpler cases.
- HIPAA usually does not apply to patient-hired advocates, which removes compliance as a forcing purchase driver.

### Cited Findings
- **Gig platforms.**
  - A ZipRecruiter listing for a Solace advocate role (third-party and unconfirmed) describes a "remote 1099 contractor role with $20–$25 per hour," requiring at least 20 hrs/week. — [ZipRecruiter Solace listing](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Solace-Rn-Healthcare-Advocate/-in-Houston,TX)
  - Understood Care posted a part-time role at $25/hr. — [Understood Care via Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/understoodcare/jobs/4109765009)
  - Solace says its advocates are "fully covered by Medicare and many Medicare Advantage programs." — [Solace](https://www.solace.health/care/a-better-healthcare-experience)
- **Part-time employee wages.** As of Jul 16, 2026, the average part-time patient-advocate wage is "$20.67" per hour. — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Part-Time-Patient-Advocate)
- **Income variability.** "Independent advocates have no guaranteed business, and a slow year will mean a lower income." The source is older and undated. — [Vault](https://legacy.vault.com/industries-professions/professions/h/health-advocates/earnings)
- **Free directory competition.** GNA is "always free for advocates." — [GNA](https://gnanow.org/professionals-join-here.html)
- **Free and freemium AI tools for consumers (2025).**
  - Counterforce Health offers a free appeal-letter generator, grant-backed, with an AI assistant ("Maxwell") that follows up with insurers. Its founder says it does not track outcomes. — [Wikipedia: Counterforce Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterforce_Health); [InsuranceNewsNet](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/new-ai-powered-programs-offer-help-to-patients-stymied-by-insurance-denials); [Spectrum News (Aug 19, 2025)](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nc/charlotte/news/2025/08/19/fighting-fire-with-fire)
  - Sheer Health's basic service (AI plus humans) is free. Users pay for help disputing denials or out-of-network reimbursement. — [Union Democrat](https://www.uniondemocrat.com/lifestyle/article_010ede69-76d6-47d5-8a40-f48f9d82fcba.html)
  - Fight Health Insurance is another free AI appeal tool. — [medicalbillrescue.com](https://medicalbillrescue.com/resources/automated-medical-debt-tools)
- **Funded consumer competitors.** Resolve raised a $3.3M seed round (July 2022) and claims "nearly $20M" in medical debt relieved at "60-65% savings" (company claim). — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220718005136/en/Resolve-Raises-3.3M-in-Seed-Funding)
- **HIPAA as a driver (compliance-vendor blogs).**
  - "A patient's independent decision to hire an advocate does not, on its own, create a business associate relationship."
  - A BAA is needed when a covered entity engages the advocate firm for billing or claims functions.
  - Sources: [AccountableHQ](https://www.accountablehq.com/post/business-associate-agreement-requirements-for-patient-billing-advocate-firms); [BCLP, "The HIPAA Trap Part 2"](https://www.bclplaw.com/print/v2/content/1562984/the-hipaa-trap-part-2-are-you-actually-a-business-associate.pdf)
- **Contingency-fee disputes.** Consumer guides warn that guaranteed savings are a red flag and that the definition of "billable savings" should be agreed in writing. This suggests savings documentation and audit trails are a real advocate need. — [lowerhospitalbill.com](https://lowerhospitalbill.com/medical-bill-advocate/)

### Inferences
- Gig-platform advocates earning $20–$25/hr use the platform's own tools and will not buy SaaS. Only owners of independent practices are buyers, and that group is likely a few thousand people at most. The APHA and GNA directories are the best proxy for its size, but I found no counts.
- AI tools hollow out the low end (simple denials and appeals), pushing human advocates toward complex cases. A tool positioned as "AI for professionals on complex cases" may age better than a letter generator.
- Because HIPAA mostly doesn't apply, selling on "HIPAA-compliant" may not drive purchases. Privacy is still a trust factor, since client documents contain PHI.

### Gaps
- No churn data for advocate memberships or tools.
- No count of active independent advocates. GNA and APHA directory sizes were not retrievable.
- No measured share of advocates who work part-time or as a hobby. The 2016 APHA survey likely has this, but its figures were not accessible.
- No evidence of consolidation among independent advocate practices.

## Alternative buyers who might pay more

### Takeaway
Employer-sponsored navigation and advocacy vendors operate at far larger revenue scale (third-party estimates of about $2.25 PEPM for Health Advocate and roughly $50–$150 PEPM for Accolade). They are more likely to build or buy enterprise tooling than solo advocates are. However, they are concentrated, slow-buying enterprises, and Accolade is now part of Transcarent.

### Cited Findings
- **Accolade.** It does not publish PEPM pricing, and pricing is quote-based. An RFP.wiki estimate is about "$50 to $150 PEPM," and is labelled an estimate. — [hrtechsaas.com](https://hrtechsaas.com/blog/included-health-vs-accolade-vs-quantum-health/); [RFP.wiki HealthJoy vs Accolade (2026)](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare/health-navigation-solutions/healthjoy/accolade)
- **Accolade ownership.** "Accolade is now part of Transcarent." — [RFP.wiki Accolade vs Transcarent (2026)](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare/health-navigation-solutions/accolade/transcarent)
- **Health Advocate.** A historical partner example points to "about $2.25 PEPM." Customization adds about $0.50 PEPM. — [RFP.wiki Health Advocate vs Included Health (2026)](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/healthcare-life-sciences/healthcare/health-navigation-solutions/health-advocate/included-health)
- **Venture-backed advocate marketplaces.** Solace bills Medicare for advocacy, which creates a funded institutional buyer that might license tooling. — [Solace](https://www.solace.health/care/a-better-healthcare-experience); [Fundable15 Substack on Solace](https://thefundable15.substack.com/p/f15-black-brief-drop-solace-health)

### Inferences
- Navigation vendors, Medicare-reimbursed advocate platforms (Solace, Understood Care, Carewell) and medical-debt startups (Resolve) employ many advocates and benefit directly from per-case efficiency. They are likelier B2B buyers than solo advocates, though they may build in-house.
- Medical-debt and consumer attorneys are an untested segment. They bill at higher rates and are accustomed to paying for legal SaaS (Clio, about $39–$129/mo, from training knowledge and not verified this session).

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on whether navigation vendors buy third-party bill-audit or coding tools versus building their own.
- I did not research attorney or revenue-cycle-consultant spending on bill-audit tools.
- No Quantum Health pricing was found.
