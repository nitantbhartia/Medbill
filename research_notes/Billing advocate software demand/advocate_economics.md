# Business Model and Economics of Independent US Medical Billing Advocates

Research date: 2026-11-16. **Method note:** WebFetch was blocked in this session (sites such as resolvemedicalbills.com, help.goodbill.com, ziprecruiter.com and careroute.ai did not resolve because they are outside the allowed network domains). All findings below come from search-result snippets rather than full-page reads, so treat exact wording as unverified. Many pricing figures come from vendor or competitor blogs that have an interest in the answer, and these are flagged as such. Dates are given where the source or snippet showed one. "Undated" means none was visible.

## 1. How advocates charge clients (hourly, contingency, flat, retainer)

### Takeaway
Independent billing advocates use three main models, often side by side. The first is contingency, typically 25-35% of savings, with a wider range of 15-50%. The second is hourly, typically $100-200/hr, with a wider range of $70-500. The third is a flat fee per task, from $75-400 for a simple review up to $300-1,500 for a bounded task. Some advocates charge monthly retainers ($200-1,000/month). Contingency is described as the most common model for bills specifically.

### Cited Findings
- KFF Health News quotes an advocacy group president who suggests about 30% contingency, and says charges range from 15% to 50% (article undated in the snippet; it is an older evergreen "advocate sidebar") — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/advocate-sidebar)
- WebMD gives contingency as typically 15-33% and hourly as about $125-150 (undated) — [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/a-to-z-guides/features/cm/help-for-medical-billing-errors-advocates)
- A consumer guide says contingency is the most common model at 25-35%, hourly is $100-200, and flat fees are $75-400 depending on complexity (2025-2026 search snippet, vendor guide) — [CareRoute](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/medical-bill-negotiation-cost); [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/when-do-you-need-medical-billing-advocate/)
- Independent advocates charge "$75-$300 per hour or $200-$1,000 per month on retainer, with most independent advocates in the $100-$200 range". Nurse-credentialed advocates charge $150-250/hr (2026, vendor blog) — [Baba](https://www.baba.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-patient-advocate/)
- An independent advocacy firm's fee FAQ says independent patient advocates charge $75-250 hourly depending on services. I attribute this from the search snippet and could not verify it on the page (undated) — [Seattle Patient Advocates fees FAQ](https://seattlepatientadvocates.com/fees)
- Flat fees run $300-1,500 for a bounded task (one bill review or one appeal letter). Some firms charge an upfront deposit of $249-499 (2025-2026 vendor blogs) — [Pinnie](https://www.pinnie.com/articles/how-much-does-a-medical-billing-advocate-cost); [Lower Hospital Bill](https://lowerhospitalbill.com/medical-bill-advocate-cost/)
- Vault describes independent advocates charging a flat $75-150+ to analyze insurance statements, plus a 50/50 split of any savings found (undated, likely older) — [Vault](https://legacy.vault.com/industries-professions/professions/h/health-advocates/earnings)
- Contingency is usually 25-35% of what is cut from the bill. The initial consultation is usually free (undated) — [Look for Zebras](https://lookforzebras.com/medical-billing-advocate-is-it-worthy-to-become-one/)
- Advocate fees for Medicare issues are $100-500/hr or $300-1,500 flat (2026, vendor) — [UnderstoodCare](https://understoodcare.com/care-guides/how-much-does-a-private-patient-advocate-cost-and-is-it-worth-it-for-medicare-issues/)
- APHA (Alliance of Professional Health Advocates) charges $125/hr (5-hour bundle) or $150/hr ad hoc for mentoring new advocates. This gives a reference point for what the trade body treats as a professional hourly rate (undated) — [APHA Mentoring](https://aphadvocates.org/Apha-mentoring-program/)

### Inferences
- A realistic central estimate for a solo billing advocate is $100-175/hr, or 25-35% contingency.
- A $99/month subscription costs roughly one billable hour. A $499/month subscription costs roughly 3-5 billable hours, or about one typical contingency case (see Section 2).

### Gaps
- I could not read individual advocate pricing pages directly because fetching was blocked. A rate-card sample of 10-20 GNA-listed billing advocates would make this much stronger.
- I found no survey-based distribution of fee models, such as the share of advocates who are contingency-only versus hourly.

## 2. Cases per month and average savings per case

### Takeaway
I found no survey of caseload. Only rough proxies exist. One comparison site reports about $1,500 saved on an average successful case. Resolve reports about 60-67% average reduction and a 70% success rate. At 25-35% contingency, a typical case therefore yields roughly $375-525 in fee revenue, though large hospital bills yield far more.

### Cited Findings
- About $1,500 saved on an average successful case (2026 snippet; CareRoute is a competing service) — [Resolve/CareRoute search snippet via CareRoute comparison](https://www.careroute.ai/bill-defense/compare-medical-bill-negotiation-services)
- Resolve's own pages variously claim average savings of 67%, 65% and about 60% of the bill, with a 70% success rate. A comparison site says the roughly 60% figure is not independently verified (2025-2026) — [Resolve How We Work](https://www.resolvemedicalbills.com/how-we-work); [ClaimsMaximizer review](https://www.claimsmaximizer.com/blog/resolve-medical-bills-review)
- CoPatient historically reported about 20% average savings (comparison site, undated) — [CoveredUSA comparison](https://coveredusa.org/en/blog/medical-bill-negotiation-services-compared)
- One nurse-side-business article describes an advocacy business with more than $1.5M in annual revenue and seven part-time advocates. That article is about 5.8 years old, so it dates to around 2020, and it is a single anecdote — [search snippet, nurse side-hustle articles](https://nursejournal.org/articles/side-gigs-for-nurses/) (exact host article uncertain)

### Inferences
- If a part-time solo advocate takes 3-8 cases per month at about $400-600 in fees each, monthly revenue would be about $1,200-4,800. This is my estimate, not sourced data.
- Savings per case are highly skewed: one large hospital or ER bill can be worth tens of thousands of dollars. Per-case revenue is lumpy, and that argues for low fixed costs.

### Gaps
- I found no published caseload figures (cases per month) from any association, survey or advocate.
- I found no median dollar savings per case from an independent source.

## 3. Annual revenue / income of a solo billing advocate

### Takeaway
I found no association survey of independent advocate income (from PACB, GNA, APHA/AdvoConnection or Trisha Torrey) in search results. The available proxies are salaries for employed roles (about $42-48K/year) and modeled estimates. Income for independent advocates is described as highly variable, with no guaranteed business.

### Cited Findings
- ZipRecruiter gives a "Medical Billing Advocate" average annual pay of $42,673 for employed roles (2026 page) — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Medical-Billing-Advocate-Salary)
- ZipRecruiter gives a "Patient Advocate" average of $20.67/hr. Indeed gives $22.93/hr, with part-time postings often at $15-20/hr (2026) — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Patient-Advocate-Salary); [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career/patient-advocate/salaries)
- Salary.com gives a patient advocate median base of $47,646 (25th-75th percentile: $43,190-54,060) as of April 2025. Zippia gives an average of $36,325 — [Salary.com](https://salary.com/research/salary/posting/patient-advocate-salary); [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/salaries/patient-advocate)
- Vault says independent advocates have no guaranteed business, and a slow year means lower income (undated) — [Vault](https://legacy.vault.com/industries-professions/professions/h/health-advocates/earnings)
- A financial-model site headlines "$120k pay" for patient advocacy owners (modeled, not surveyed; low credibility) — [FinancialModelsLab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/how-much-makes/patient-advocacy)
- Trisha Torrey's *Health Advocate's Start and Grow Your Own Practice Handbook* covers "can you make a living" and pricing, but its figures were not available in search results — [DiagKNOWsis Media](https://diagknowsismedia.com/books/start-and-grow-practice/)
- Adjacent benchmark: median solo private-practice therapist revenue was $75,000 in 2025, with $52,663 profit (Heard 2026 report, n=1,950). This is a different profession and serves only as a rough ceiling for solo health-services practices — [Heard](https://www.joinheard.com/resources/the-heard-2026-financial-state-of-private-practice-report)

### Inferences
- Solo independent billing advocates plausibly gross between about $20K (part-time) and $100K (established full-time). This is my estimate, bounded by the employed-salary and therapist proxies above.
- A $99/month tool costs $1,188/year, which is about 1-6% of that revenue range. A $499/month tool costs $5,988/year, which is 6-30%. That is a heavy load at the low end.

### Gaps
- I found no PACB, GNA, APHA or AdvoConnection member income survey in search results. Any such data is likely members-only.
- I found no Reddit first-person income reports.

## 4. Share that are part-time, side businesses, or retirees/former nurses

### Takeaway
I found no quantitative data. Qualitative sources consistently describe the field as a second career or side business for nurses, and as a mix of part-time practitioners.

### Cited Findings
- Nurse side-hustle guides list patient and billing advocacy as a side business. Nurses can contract with established advocacy groups or start their own business, with fees of 20-30% of savings (2025-2026) — [NurseJournal](https://nursejournal.org/articles/side-gigs-for-nurses/); [Credenza Health](https://credenzahealth.com/career-advice/37-great-side-hustles-for-nurses/)
- The one sizable advocacy business cited ($1.5M revenue) was staffed by seven *part-time* advocates (anecdote, about 2020) — same source as Section 2
- Nurse-credentialed advocates command higher rates ($150-250/hr vs. $100-200 typical) (2026) — [Baba](https://www.baba.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-patient-advocate/)
- The professional base is small. One article describes the first PACB cohort as "one of only 149 Board Certified Patient Advocates" (undated, around 2018-2019) — [Mercer](https://www.mercer.us/our-thinking/healthcare/michael-garrett-joins-first-cohort-of-board-certified-patient-advocates.html)
- GNA maintains an "Insurance & Billing" specialty directory filter. I could not obtain a count — [GNA directory](https://gnanow.org/advocates?search_type=speciality_id&speciality_id=16)

### Inferences
- The market of independent billing advocates is likely small, in the low thousands at most, and skews toward part-time and second-career practitioners. That is a price-sensitive segment.

### Gaps
- I found no survey data on the share of advocates who are part-time, retirees or former nurses.
- I found no current count of BCPA holders or GNA billing-specialty listings. Counting the GNA filter manually would answer the second.

## 5. Startup costs and operating expenses

### Takeaway
Only modeled or blog estimates exist. A solo, home-based launch is estimated at $2K-15K. The main recurring costs are E&O/professional liability insurance (about $500-2,500/yr), certification ($425 BCPA exam), directory memberships and modest marketing. Software is mentioned but never quantified.

### Cited Findings
- Professional liability (E&O) costs about $700-2,500/yr in one guide and $500-2,000/yr in another. Cyber liability costs about $2,000-5,000/yr (2025-2026 blog/model estimates) — [Startup Financial Projection](https://startupfinancialprojection.com/blogs/capex/patient-advocacy); [Workhint](https://blog.workhint.com/blog/start-patient-advocacy-business-no-staff/)
- Total solo startup costs are estimated at $2,000-10,000 in one source and $5,000-15,000 in another (home-based, covering certification, insurance, registration and initial marketing). Initial marketing is estimated at $200-1,000 (2025-2026) — [Startup Financial Projection](https://startupfinancialprojection.com/blogs/opening/patient-advocacy); [Workhint](https://blog.workhint.com/blog/start-patient-advocacy-business-no-staff/)
- A modeled home-based solo practice has monthly operating expenses of $1,600-7,450, excluding owner pay. The PACB application and exam fee is $425 (model, low credibility for expenses; the exam fee is plausible) — [FinancialModelsLab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/business-insights/patient-advocacy); [PACB Get Certified](https://www.pacboard.org/get-certified/)
- BCPA must be renewed every 3 years, which is an ongoing cost — [PACB Stay Certified](https://www.pacboard.org/stay-certified/)

### Inferences
- Current software spend is likely minimal, mostly generic tools such as Google Workspace, a PDF editor and a CRM or spreadsheet. A $99-199/month specialized tool would likely be the advocate's largest software line item.

### Gaps
- I found no first-hand reports of what advocates actually spend on software. A user interview or survey would be needed.
- I found no GNA or APHA membership fee figures in the results (I believe these are low hundreds per year, but this is unverified).

## 6. B2B / venture-backed billing-advocacy firms and their models

### Takeaway
The venture-backed players use either consumer contingency (Resolve 20-25% with minimums; Goodbill 20% capped at $1,000; CoPatient historically 35%) or have moved to employer-paid or plan-paid models (Goodbill's pivot to self-funded employers and TPAs; Medical Cost Advocate via employer benefits, now part of Emry/Goodroot). This suggests consumer contingency alone is hard to scale, and that employer channels carry the larger firms.

### Cited Findings
- **Resolve:** For bills under $15,000, the fee is 25% of savings with a $1,000 minimum fee. For bills of $15,000 or more, the success fee is 20%. "You never pay more than you save." Third-party sites describe a $499 refundable deposit and a 10% fee tier for larger bills, which conflicts with the figures above (2025-2026) — [Resolve FAQ](https://resolvemedicalbills.com/faqs/how-much-does-it-cost-to-work-with-resolve); [Resolve FAQ](https://resolvemedicalbills.com/faq); [ClaimsMaximizer](https://www.claimsmaximizer.com/blog/resolve-medical-bills-review)
- **Resolve:** Has partner channels (GoodRx, financial-wellness partners, KonnectMD), which points to B2B2C distribution — [Resolve/GoodRx](https://www.resolvemedicalbills.com/lp/goodrx); [Resolve partners](https://www.resolvemedicalbills.com/partners/konnectmd)
- **Goodbill:** Charges 20% of savings only on settlement, capped at $1,000 (help page, current as of search) — [Goodbill Help](https://help.goodbill.com/en/articles/254209)
- **Goodbill:** Raised $3.4M (2022) and $2M (April 2024). It shifted to serving self-funded employers and TPAs by vetting hospital claims, covering about 50,000 people as of 2024 — [GeekWire 2022](https://www.geekwire.com/2022/the-darkest-most-bottomless-pit-in-healthcare-goodbill-raises-3-4m-to-tackle-medical-billing/); [GeekWire 2024](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/goodbill-raises-2m-and-expands-customer-base-to-unearth-and-challenge-medical-billing-errors/); [FinSMEs 2024](https://www.finsmes.com/2024/04/goodbill-raises-2m-in-funding.html)
- **CoPatient:** Earned revenue by taking 35% of savings (2014). Current comparison sites cite 30% or 35%, which conflict and are unverified. It partnered with Triage Cancer (2021) — [GeekWire 2014](https://www.geekwire.com/2014/copatient-raises-3-6m/); [PR Newswire 2021](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/triage-cancer--copatient-partner-to-help-individuals-diagnosed-with-cancer-review-manage-and-negotiate-their-medical-bills-301262917.html)
- **Medical Cost Advocate (MCA):** Founded in 2001 in Wyckoff, NJ. It is sold to employers, for example as an add-on to HealthPass small-employer plans. It merged into Goodroot's Emry Health in August 2024. Emry claims savings of more than $1,700 per employee per year (vendor claim). I found no published PEPM fee — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/medical-cost-advocate); [PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/healthpass-enhances-health-plan-offering-for-small-employers/)
- **Nonprofit alternative:** Dollar For (charity-care applications, free) competes for the same consumers, as does the Patient Advocate Foundation (free case management for eligible patients) — [CoveredUSA comparison](https://coveredusa.org/en/blog/medical-bill-negotiation-services-compared); [Health Bill Central](https://healthbillcentral.com/blog/medical-bill-advocates-guide)

### Inferences
- Firms that employ teams of advocates (Resolve, Goodbill, MCA/Emry) are more credible buyers for a $299-499/month or per-seat tool than solo advocates are, though they may build their own tooling.
- Overall affordability judgment: established full-time solo advocates could justify about $99-199/month if the tool saves 1-2 billable hours per month or wins one extra case. $499/month is likely affordable only for multi-advocate firms or very high-volume solos. Part-time and side-business advocates, who appear to be a large share, are likely to resist anything above about $50-99/month or would prefer per-case pricing. This is my inference from the fee and income ranges above.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm current pricing on official CoPatient or MCA/Emry pages.
- I found no data on employer per-employee-per-month (PEPM) pricing for billing advocacy.
- I could not determine Goodbill's or CoPatient's status as of 2025-2026; no shutdown or acquisition news was found.
